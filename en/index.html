01 / About

About me

My research focuses on conceptual representation and the cognitive processes involved in language.

I earned my PhD in Clinical and Health Psychology at the Autonomous University of Madrid (2024) and am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Behavioral Sciences Methodology at UNED. My doctoral thesis, De vectores a conceptos: nuevas rutas en la representación computacional del lenguaje, laid the groundwork for much of my current research.

I work with semantic representations, vector subspaces, and language models. I study what these representations preserve about concepts and whether their properties correspond to human judgments and behavior.

An important part of this work is comparing models with simpler alternatives and examining the validity of the measures derived from them. Negative results and limits to generalization are informative, too.

02 / Research

Research

My current work combines computational modeling, behavioral research, and methodological evaluation.

Research direction in development

Beyond Point Representation (BPR)

When does representing a concept as a subspace preserve information that is lost when it is reduced to a single point or vector? This work examines semantic geometry, context, and learning, and compares quantum-inspired models with classical alternatives.

Conceptual representation · Cognitive modeling
Funded project · 2025–2028

AGRUPA

I collaborate on the computational study of social stereotypes in cultural artifacts and descriptions of artworks, focusing on the measurement of psychological constructs and the analysis of textual data.

Working team · PID2024-157794NB-I00 · UAM
Research direction

Language and psychological measurement

I study what language models can infer from text responses and when such indicators can be considered valid and generalizable. Recent work examines surprisal as an individual-difference measure.

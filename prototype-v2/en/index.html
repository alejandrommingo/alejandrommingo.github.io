Why can reversing a comparison change the judgment?

In the classical similarity example, “North Korea is similar to China” and “China is similar to North Korea” need not lead to equivalent judgments.

Before the comparison

We begin from an initial state that does not favor either concept. A directed similarity comparison follows.

About this example and its limits

This illustration draws on Tversky’s (1977) similarity asymmetry and the projection model by Pothos, Busemeyer, and Trueblood (2013). A line represents North Korea and a plane represents China. The geometry and numerical values are illustrative, not fitted to individual data. Each selected projection updates the state; its later dissolution is a visual metaphor.

Current research areas

My work connects conceptual models, the evaluation of language models, and methodological collaborations across psychology and clinical research.

01 / RESEARCH AREA

Conceptual representation and cognition

What do computational representations preserve or discard about concepts? I study semantic subspaces, contextual changes and quantum-inspired geometrical models against classical alternatives.
